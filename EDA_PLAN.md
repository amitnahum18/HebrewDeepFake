## Observed structure
The workspace initially held an empty `dataset.ipynb`. The repository contains a manifest and one directory per session with `audio.mka`, `metadata.json`, `transcript.vtt`, and `transcript.aligned.json`. Manifest fields cover session/speaker IDs, seconds, document/source/language, birth year, biological sex, alignment quality, segment and word counts, and summary lengths/rates. The JSON metadata additionally has per-segment alignment scores. Aligned transcripts contain segment/word timing and probability; VTT timestamps come from recital interactions and are not ground truth alignment.

## Analysis sequence and acceptance criteria
1. **Acquire and freeze inputs.** Finish the exact requested CLI download; record revision, file inventory, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes. Record software versions, seed, run time, and local input path. Count expected/actual companion files and retain failures explicitly.
2. **Validate schema and joins.** Build recording, speaker, aligned segment, word, and per-segment quality tables. Check duplicate IDs, missing/orphan sessions, manifest/JSON field agreement, missing values and sentinel values, types, nonpositive durations, impossible numeric ranges, invalid birth years, out-of-bounds/reversed/overlapping timestamps, alignment count mismatches, and language inconsistencies. Keep raw fields alongside derived fields.
3. **Describe every manifest feature.** Export data dictionary, categorical counts including unknown values, numerical percentiles/IQR/MAD and histograms. Report total sessions/hours/words/segments, distinct speakers and documents, source composition, duration and rate distributions. Distinguish reported counts from recomputed transcript counts.
4. **Assess demographic coverage and balance.** Report biological sex and birth-year coverage and inconsistencies within speaker; do not infer gender, age, or accent from audio. Age at recording is unavailable without reliable recording dates. Use age-in-reference-year only if clearly labeled, with no claim about age at recording. Show speaker-, recording-, and duration-weighted distributions, age-proxy bins, and sex × age-proxy cells. Unknown is a visible category. Show contribution concentration, Lorenz curve, Gini, top-1/5/10 speaker shares, and effective speaker count based on hours. Equal shares are a descriptive benchmark, not a population target.
5. **Audit transcripts and alignment.** Preserve original Hebrew and add versioned Unicode/whitespace/niqqud/punctuation normalization for matching. Describe Hebrew/Latin/digit/niqqud presence, vocabulary and token frequencies, text length, repeated sessions/segments and texts shared across speakers, document repetition, words/minute using full recording and aligned time separately, word probabilities, and timing coverage/gaps. Compare VTT and aligned text after normalization. Automatic alignment confidence is not WER or acoustic quality.
6. **Inspect audio for all sessions.** Decode with PyAV while preserving source files. Record codec, sample rate, channels, decoded duration, sample peak, RMS dBFS, DC offset, near-full-scale sample fraction, frame-level quiet fraction, and energy-based dynamic-range/noise proxy. Compare decoded/manifest durations. Use 20 ms frames, document thresholds, and distinguish quiet audio from silence and dynamic range from true SNR. Report decode failures and nonfinite samples. Hash source bytes and decoded PCM to detect exact/canonical duplicates; these do not establish all perceptual duplicates. Avoid unvalidated claims of speech activity, perceived quality, or multi-speaker presence.
7. **Investigate associations and outliers.** Flag modified Z > 3.5 where MAD > 0; use IQR when MAD is zero. Add explicit technical and alignment screening rules with denominators. Compare quality/duration/noise proxies across demographic, source, and codec groups, show cell sizes and speaker-level summaries, and use descriptive correlations. No causal claims or recording-level significance tests that treat repeated speakers as independent. Keep flags separate from exclusion decisions. Export ranked review candidates and a seeded speaker-spanning baseline listening sample, with paths/timestamps for human follow-up.
8. **Assess leakage and downstream suitability.** Audit speaker and exact normalized text connectivity across sessions, document largest speaker/text component and split feasibility. Do not invent train/test assignments. Describe source/channel confounds relevant to HebSpoof and limits of read speech, missing demographics, and automated QC. No new synthetic or deepfake data are generated.
9. **Deliver and verify.** Populate and execute `dataset.ipynb` from a clean kernel; save visible tables and plots plus reusable CSV/JSON outputs and a concise Markdown findings/progress report. Verify zero cell errors, output counts, source/analysis coverage, and inspect representative figures. Every unavailable check and unresolved interpretation must be explicit. No synthetic rows or substitute dataset.

## Screening rules (exploratory, not automatic exclusion)
- Provider guidance: session alignment score < 0.3; segment score < 0.5.
- Technical: nonpositive duration; timestamp violation with 0.1 s tolerance; duration discrepancy > max(0.25 s, 1%); nonfinite audio; peak >= 0.999 fraction > 0.1%; quiet frame fraction > 50% at -45 dBFS; RMS < -40 dBFS; absolute DC > 0.01.
- Compare quiet fraction at -50/-45/-40 dBFS to expose threshold sensitivity. Lossy decoded peaks can exceed full scale; peak screening is only a clipping candidate.
- Numerical outliers remain review candidates; long sessions and unusual vocabulary may be valid. Missingness stays visible in all denominators.
